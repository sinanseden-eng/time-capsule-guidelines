A Time Capsule for 2225

Your mission: curate a message for the future. Choose wisely what stories and objects will define our era.

1

First Check: Defining Your Core Message

Your Task

  • Understand the purpose and challenges of creating a time capsule.

  • Brainstorm 2-3 potential "core messages" about our current era (e.g., technology, environment, culture).

  • Choose one key idea you want to communicate to the people of 2225.

Expectations

By the end of our meeting, you will have a clear, focused, and approved "core message" that will serve as the foundation for your entire project.

Outline Prep: Questions to Consider

Before our second meeting, think through these questions. They will help you build the outline for your report and choose the most impactful items for your capsule.

The Big Picture (Your Introduction):

What is the single most important story about today that you want people in 2225 to understand? How will your report's introduction explain this core message and the purpose of your specific capsule?

The Items (Your Body Paragraphs):

For each potential item, ask: Does this object tell a story on its own? How does it connect directly to my core message? If you could only pick one item to represent your entire message, which would it be, and why?

The Future (Your Analysis):

How could an item be completely misunderstood? What context or knowledge might be lost over 200 years? (e.g., They might think a smartphone was just a decorative black rock.) How will you use speculative language to explore these possibilities?

2

Second Check: Item Selection & Research

Your Task

  • Brainstorm a list of potential items (physical, digital, etc.) for your capsule.

  • Select the final 5–7 items that best represent your core message.

  • Begin outlining your justification for each item and your overall report.

Expectations

You will present your final list of 5-7 items. We will discuss their relevance to your core message and ensure your choices are strong and defensible.

3

Third Check: Justification Draft

Your Task

  • Write a full rough draft of the justification for each item.

  • For each item, explain what it is, its significance to your core message, and how it might be interpreted or misunderstood.

  • Practice using speculative language (e.g., "they might think," "it must have been") to discuss future interpretations.

Expectations

I will provide detailed feedback on your analysis, focusing on the depth of your justifications and your correct use of speculative language.

4

Fourth Check: Final Report & Product Plan

Your Task

  • Revise your draft and submit the final, polished written report.

  • Decide on your final product format (physical, digital, or report).

  • Create a detailed plan or storyboard for your presentation/product.

Expectations

I will collect your final written report. We will review your product plan and schedule your final presentation date.

5

Fifth Check: Final Presentation

Your Task

  • Finalize your time capsule product (physical model, digital presentation, or report).

  • Practice your presentation to ensure it is clear, confident, and within the time limit.

  • Present your time capsule and its contents to the class.

Expectations

A polished and engaging presentation that clearly communicates your core message and demonstrates a deep, analytical approach to your selections.

Project Rules & Grading

General Rules

  • No plagiarism! You must reference all sources.
  • Stick to the check days and the deadline (5 check-ins required).
  • Be ready to present your work orally in class.
  • All work must be your own product without outside help.
  • Papers must be presentable, tidy, and well-written (Calibri, 11pt if typed). Submit in a file with your name, number, and class.
